Start in three steps.

  1. Choose a history shortcut. Set a comfortable combination at first launch. The default is ⇧⌘V (Shift–Command–V); change it in Settings. Use another combination if it conflicts with an existing shortcut.
  2. Agree to start recording, then make a new copy. Select text and press ⌘C. Earlier clipboard content is not imported retroactively.
  3. Open history from the destination field. Click where you want to paste and press your shortcut. A single click previews a record. Double-click or select it and press Enter to use it.

The menu bar clipboard icon also opens history. Enable Show app in Dock in Settings for a Dock icon. Choose English, 한국어, or the system language in Settings → General → Language.

Is a new copy missing?

  1. Check that the app says Recording. If it was paused, resume recording and copy again.
  2. Clear the search and choose All history. Switch from compact to full view to check groups.
  3. Work mode at the lower left controls the group for future copies. Choose Record without a group if copies are going into an unintended group. Existing items remain in All history.
  4. Check excluded apps, sensitive-item settings, and any macOS clipboard-access notice. Recording access and direct-paste permission are separate.
  5. Copy the text or image itself. Files copied in Finder are not recorded in this version. If you see a storage-limit message, review unneeded records and pins.

Search covers the first 8 KB of text, plus titles and groups. Image OCR search is not included; organize images with titles and groups.

Paste with double-click or Enter

Enable direct insertion with double-click and Enter in Settings. Use Setup & permissions to check Accessibility access for the installed app. Click the destination field → press your history shortcut → double-click a record, or select it and press Enter.

The window closes, but nothing is inserted.

Click the destination field and reopen history. Switching apps or making a new copy after opening history cancels an in-progress delivery. Check the direct-paste option and Refresh permission status; make sure the field is editable. You can also copy the selected record and press ⌘V in the destination.

I prefer to copy and return.

Turn direct insertion off. Copy in a record’s context menu always copies without inserting. Press ⌘V in the app that should receive the content.

Search, editing, and copying work without Accessibility access. Complete macOS password or Touch ID prompts directly; do not send credentials to support.

Edit an image before you paste

  1. Copy an image, then click the document or app where it should be inserted.
  2. Open history and choose Edit image for that record. In compact view, use the record’s context menu.
  3. Choose crop, pen, arrow, shape, text, or redaction. Undo, Redo, and Reset to original are available.
  4. Choose Edit and paste, or Copy edited image followed by ⌘V in the destination. The original record stays intact; the edited result is delivered as PNG.
Text is copied instead of the image.

Preview may have selected recognized text. Choose Tools → Rectangular Selection, select the image area, and copy it. Copying image pixels is different from copying the file in Finder.

Color and width are disabled.

Select a tool that uses them, such as pen, arrow, shape, or text. Crop boundaries use a fixed visible width. Redaction fills the selected area with opaque black.

How do I save a favorite color?

Use the color picker or apply a six-digit #RRGGBB code, then choose Save color with the star. Click a saved swatch to reuse it. Save up to 24 colors; the last selected color is remembered. New colors and widths apply to new marks, leaving existing marks unchanged.

The destination does not show the edited image.

The destination must support pasted PNG images. Plain-text fields cannot display them. For a simple check, use a rich-text TextEdit document. Move the editor by its title bar; another app can appear in front when you switch to it.

Make the compact window your own

Enable compact mode in Settings → History window. Turn on the live window preview to see width, height, and opacity changes as you make them. The sliders are available while compact mode is enabled.

Drag the preview’s title bar to move it; use the red close button to hide it. Enable the preview option again to reopen it. Reposition preview brings it back to an easy-to-find position. Switch the real history window to full view for detailed previews and groups.

Pins, groups, and multiple-record deletion

Pin frequent clips and organize them into groups. Shift-click selects a range; Command-click selects individual records. After deleting a selection, use the displayed Undo action or ⌘Z while the history list has focus to restore the latest deletion batch.

Undo for the latest deletion batch is available for 10 seconds. Clear all history and a complete storage reset cannot be restored this way. Do not use a storage reset as the first remedy for an ordinary recording or permission issue.

Manage recording on your Mac

Text, images, and names you add are encrypted locally. History is not automatically uploaded to an app-operated server or AI service. Operational metadata such as timestamps and sizes is not encrypted. Default limits are 30 days, 5,000 records, and 512 MB total. Pins do not expire automatically but count toward the storage limit.

Pause recording before copying content you do not want saved. App exclusions and sensitive markers reduce capture but cannot detect every secret. Apply retention or exclusion changes with Save retention & exclusions.

Automatic translation, OCR, and cloud sync are not included. Mac Assistant provides eight built-in topics and favorites. Basic guides remain available without on-device topic classification.

Need more help?

Email contact@studionariz.com with your app version, macOS version, destination app, steps to reproduce, and error message. You do not need to share the original clipboard content.

View content-free diagnostics in Settings to check status. Diagnostics are saved only when you choose to save them and are never sent automatically. Remove names, contact details, and private history before attaching a screenshot. Do not send passwords, verification codes, or sensitive clipboard records.
